Preliminary evaluation · public data only

How well does it work, and compared to what?

A plain, measured account of what this tool does, how accurate it is on a starter benchmark, and how it compares with the ways this research is done today. This is a preliminary evaluation on a modest test set — the numbers will move as the test set grows and is expert-reviewed. Nothing here is a marketing claim; where the tool is weak or narrow, that is stated plainly.

Scope & disclaimer. The system covers only public North Carolina child-welfare law and policy. It is a research aid, not legal advice, and not a substitute for a licensed attorney's judgment or for a full legal-research service. Every result links to the official source, which remains the controlling text.

1 · What it does

Given a plain-English question, the tool finds the governing North Carolina authority and can write a short answer grounded in it:

  • Search — returns the most relevant statute sections and policy passages, each with a full citation (source, section, page) and a link to the official document.
  • Grounded answer — an optional short answer assembled only from the retrieved passages, with inline [n] citations. If the passages don't support an answer, it says so rather than guessing.

2 · What's in it (the corpus)

CollectionContentsScale
Statutes & regulationsNC General Statutes, Chapter 7B (the Juvenile Code) + supporting NC Administrative Code (10A NCAC 70)28 documents
Agency policyNCDHHS child-welfare policy manuals, protocols, forms, and guidance502 documents · ~4,150 pages

~18,300 indexed passages total. Every passage carries its source URL, section, page span, and a SHA-256 content hash for tamper-evidence. The statute set is curated (the Chapter 7B core and supporting sections), not the entire NC General Statutes.

3 · How it works

Documents are converted to clean text (page numbers preserved), split into passages at statutory/section boundaries, and indexed two ways: by keyword and by meaning (embeddings). A query runs against both lanes, the candidates are fused and re-ranked, and — for a written answer — the top passages are passed to a language model that is constrained to cite them and to decline when they're insufficient. The engine is walled off from any confidential record in code.

4 · Measured performance

Accuracy is measured on a starter benchmark of 70 test questions (42 statute, 28 policy) whose correct answer is known in advance. The metric that matters most is top-3 accuracy — how often the governing authority appears in the first three results.

Body of authorityTop-1Top-3Top-5
Statutes (GS Ch. 7B)73%90%92%
Policy (NCDHHS)82%96%96%

In plain terms: the correct authority is in the top three results about nine times in ten for statutes and ~19 in 20 for policy. Grounded answers were verified to produce correct, cited summaries and to decline when the sources don't support one.

Honest limitations

  • Not perfect on the first result. When the exact section isn't first, the results above it are almost always closely-related provisions (e.g., the failure-to-answer section vs. the answer section). Near-misses, not random errors — but a reader should scan the top few results, not just the first.
  • Curated corpus. Questions outside the indexed Chapter 7B + NCDHHS policy set won't resolve. Federal law, case law, and other NC statutes are out of scope.
  • Starter benchmark. 70 questions is enough to be indicative, not definitive; a larger, expert-reviewed set would sharpen (and could lower) these figures.
  • Preliminary. This is an early evaluation of a system still being tuned.

5 · Compared with business as usual

Today, answering these questions generally means one of the following. The comparison is meant to be fair — each conventional method has real strengths this tool does not.

ApproachStrengthsLimitations for this task
Manual PDF search
(open the Chapter 7B PDF / policy manuals, Ctrl-F)		Free; exact; you read the controlling text directly. Slow across 238-page statutes + ~4,150 pages of policy; keyword-only (misses synonyms and paraphrases); no cross-document search; easy to miss the governing provision.
Commercial legal databases
(Westlaw, Lexis)		 Far broader — all case law and statutes, annotations, citators, editorial analysis. The authoritative tool for legal research. Subscription cost; and critically, NCDHHS agency policy manuals are generally not indexed there — so the policy side of these questions is largely unaddressed.
General AI chatbot
(consumer LLM)		 Fast; plain-language answers. May fabricate; typically no citation to the actual controlling text; can be outdated; no guarantee it read the current NC provision.
This tool Semantic search over both statutes and agency policy in one place; every result cited to source + section + page with an integrity hash; grounded answers that decline when unsupported; public/no subscription. Narrow corpus; a research aid, not a Westlaw replacement or a lawyer; retrieval imperfect (see §4); not legal advice.
Where it is most useful. The clearest gap it fills is agency policy — the NCDHHS manuals that commercial databases don't index and that are painful to search by hand. For statutes, it's a fast first-pass finder that always points back to the official text; it does not replace authoritative legal research.

6 · What it is not

  • Not legal advice, and not a substitute for an attorney's judgment.
  • Not a comprehensive legal-research service (no case law, no citator, no federal law).
  • Not a system of record — the linked official document is always controlling.
  • Not connected to any confidential case file; it reads only public NC law and policy.

7 · Methods & reproducibility

The benchmark queries and scoring harness are versioned and re-runnable. Retrieval is scored by whether the document(s) that correctly answer a question appear in the top-k results (for policy, any of the legitimately-correct documents on a topic counts; for statutes, only the exact governing section). Figures reflect the live system on the date below and will change as the corpus and test set evolve. Chunk text has been checked to avoid mid-sentence truncation; retrieval uses a keyword + meaning hybrid with a re-ranking step.
