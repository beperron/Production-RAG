This child may have been exposed to a methamphetamine laboratory. This sheet reviews some suggestions that will help you provide safe care for the child and your household.

### Health Considerations

The child has had an initial medical assessment. Information you should know about the medical assessment including the discharge summary is attached to this form. We do not expect the child to have any additional problems. However, because some effects of chemical exposure can develop slowly, you should observe the child carefully for symptoms for the next 24 to 48 hours. Please call the medical contact number on the bottom of this sheet immediately if you notice any of the following:

- Headache, drowsiness, hallucinations or mental confusion,
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, coughing, or poor color,
- Fever,
- Unusual movements like tremors, shaking, jumpiness,

agitation or seizures; or

- Any other unusual symptom that seems severe and worries you.

### Contamination Considerations

The child may also have come into contact with chemicals or toxins that could contaminate others. Most of these chemicals are common household or automotive chemicals, but they can be toxic. The child has been given a shower and has been provided with new clothes. There are some additional steps listed below that you may take to reassure yourself.

1. Give the child a bath with very warm water (not hot) and lots of soap.

Wash completely, including hair, face, between the toes, and other hard-to-reach places. Drain the tub and give a second bath to remove any residual chemical. Afterwards, drain and clean the tub thoroughly.

2. Place child's clothes in a plastic garbage bag until they can be washed.

Wash the child's clothes separately from other clothes on the hottest setting. Rewash a second time and air dry outside the home, not in the dryer. Run the washer once empty to clean it thoroughly.

It is important to provide a warm, stable, accepting environment for the child and to understand the emotional reactions that may follow from the child's experiences.
