IN HOME FAMILY CASE PLAN (IHFCP) INSTRUCTIONS

GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS

Which Cases All cases assigned in which the family is receiving CPS In-Home Services after substantiation or a "Child Protective Services Needed" finding is made. If the DSS is granted custody, the Permanency Planning Family Case Plan is to be used, even if the child physically remains in the home. Requirements for IV-E Candidacy: Cases will be coded for Title IV-E funding when all the following are true:

• The CPS-Assessment case decision was "substantiated" or "services needed"; • The most recent Safety Assessment has a decision of "Safe with a plan" and the agency is still actively monitoring the safety interventions in that plan; • The agency will pursue custody if the danger indicators cannot be controlled by interventions; • A case plan developed with the family must be in place; these must include documented services and an imminent risk statement

o The Evidence-Based Prevention Services Plan (temporary); THEN o In-Home Family Case Plan (within 30 days) Purpose

The purpose of the In-Home Family Case Plan (DSS-5239) is to develop a plan together with the family that specifies the services to be offered or provided to the family that address the reasons the family was referred to CPS In-Home Services and the children in the family are at risk of entering foster care. The plan should build on identified strengths to address needs identified in the Family Strengths and Needs Assessment and Child Strength and Needs, when applicable and other required assessments and tools. Additionally, the In Home Family Case Plan (IHFCP):

• Documents activities agreed to with the family and the family's safety network to assure the safety of children in the family. • Meets federal requirements for documenting children who are determined to be eligible to be a Candidate or FFPSA Candidate for Foster Care. The determination for candidacy along with this plan developed WITH the family allows the county to claim administrative costs to IV-E. • Meets federal requirements in title IV-B to address well-being needs of parents, caretakers and children receiving In-Home services. Participants in Plan Development The In-Home Family Case Plan (IHFCP) form must be developed by the CPS In-Home Services caseworker or other caseworker as assigned jointly with the family. The county child welfare services agency must engage or make efforts to engage all parents and caretakers and their

Safety Networks in the process of developing the IHFCP. Children's participation in the development of the IHFCP is required. For younger or non-verbal children, caseworkers should seek to get input in developmentally appropriate ways. How children participate in developing the case plan must be documented for each child included on the case plan. The involvement of noncustodial parents and their relatives in the development of the plan must also be documented in the plan, respectively, as well as the family/case documentation. If a non-custodial parent was not involved, the caseworker's efforts to engage the parent and the reasons the parent was not involved must be documented in the family/case documentation. Families should be given the option of developing the initial IHFCP alone with the caseworker, involving necessary Safety Network members, or developing the plan within the context of a full Child and Family Team (CFT) meeting. If the plan is not developed within a CFT meeting, the completed plan and progress made must be reviewed at CFT meetings held throughout the life of services as described in the CFT policy in the Cross Function manual. The family's preference on who they want present when the plan is developed and updated should be documented in the case record. When domestic violence is involved, separate IHFCPs must be created: one with the non-offending parent or adult victim, and another with the perpetrator. The perpetrator must not have access to the non-offending parent or adult victim's plan. In some situations, the non-offending parent or adult victim may request that the perpetrator be included in a joint plan or CFT meeting. Before agreeing, the County DSS and/or facilitator must review relevant information in the case record, including any protective orders or other court orders, to determine whether meeting jointly is safe and appropriate. If the County DSS and/or facilitator determines that meeting together would pose a safety risk for the non-offending victim—or that holding a CFT meeting with the perpetrator present is unsafe—then the plans must be completed separately. Refer to the Domestic Violence protocol and guidance for additional direction. When The IHFCP must be developed within 30 days of the case decision to substantiate or find CPS Services Needed, updated every three months thereafter to coincide with the Family Strengths and Needs Assessment, Child Strengths and Needs Assessment (as applicable) and Progress to Case Closure Guide, or modified whenever family circumstances warrant a change. When timeframes for developing the initial plan or completing required updates are not met, documentation in the client record must reflect diligent efforts made or the rationale for extra time needed. Section 1: Family Information

• For each child, enter the first name, last name, current address, and date of birth • For each parent, enter the first name, last name, current address, telephone number and date of birth. • For each caretaker with whom a child lives, enter the name, current address, date of birth (DOB), and telephone number. Section 2: Behavior Change and Services to Address Needs

The top three priority needs identified through the Family Strengths and Needs Assessment (FSNA) become the basis for the objectives/goals in the In-Home Family Case Plan (IHFCP). PATH NC will auto-populate these needs from the FSNA onto the IHFCP. By explicitly naming a family's strengths and natural supports, the FSNA helps caseworkers, supervisors, and safety network members engage in a collaborative assessment process that empowers families and grounds the case plan in their own capacity to create safety. This strengths-focused approach reinforces child safety while guiding effective and meaningful case planning.

Only list needs that directly affect the child's current safety or the potential for future harm, as these needs guide the services required to complete CPS In-Home Services. If fewer than three such safety-related needs exist, leave the remaining sections blank. (Identified needs not impacting current safety or future harm can be addressed in Child Specific Well-Being Needs or Parent or Caretaker Well-Being Needs to be addressed with Optional Services.)

In the rare circumstance that more than 3 needs were identified on the Family Strengths and Needs Assessment that must be addressed by the family to complete in-home services, additional sheets may be added and completed. If so, the caseworker should:

• Consider whether a single service can effectively address more than one of the identified needs. Multiple required services may be overwhelming for the family. • Consider discussing with the family whether work on needs and participation in services can be safely and effectively sequenced. Instructions for Specific Boxes: When developing the original plan, complete the charts in Section 2 that identify the need, the behavioral change needed, and the services to be offered and provided when developing the plan with the family. Need: Document the harm and worry statements. For each caretaker domain identified as a priority need on the Family Strengths and Needs Assessment (FSNA) in PATH NC, the related need will automatically appear in this section. In the space provided, In-Home caseworkers should document the harm statement and the worry statement associated with each priority need or domain.

Imminent or Serious Risk of Foster Care This Section addresses whether a child is determined to be eligible to be a Candidate or FFPSA Candidate for Foster Care and a separate page should be used for each child. This chart is designed to ensure IHFCP required documentation for counties to properly claim administrative costs for Candidates for Foster Care. In the first column a check box should be marked to indicate whether the child is at serious or imminent risk of removal and placement into Foster Care. This information comes directly off the Candidacy Determination Form completed separately from the family. If no, skip to Section 6. If yes is marked, then a behaviorally specific explanation of the concerns that place the child at serious or imminent risk of removal must be completed and explained to the family. The third column must include the services that the family agrees to engage in to mitigate the risks to the child. An update on the progress of services should be completed every 90 days. Any changes to the determination or services must be documented in the update boxes, but if no change the caseworker can indicate "no change" or N/A in the determination explanation and service boxes. Policy regarding Candidacy for Foster Care can be found in the In-Home Manual (PATH NC). Because a determination of foster care candidacy for children whose families are actively receiving an Evidenced Based Program (EBP) funded by Title IV-E and included in North Carolina's Title IV- E Prevention Services Plan as allowable under the Family First Prevention Services Act (FFPSA) is good for 12 months, the chart does not need to be updated until the completion of the service up to 12 months. A list of available EBPs funded by Title IV-E in North Carolina's Title IV-E Prevention Services Plan is located at https://www.ncdhhs.gov/nc-family-first-prevention-plan/download?attachment and will be updated as new services become available.

The fourth row addresses any mental health treatment being provided to the child. It should also include any needs that are not safety related for the child regarding concerns or an assessment based on observed behaviors or the results of the NC Trauma Screening Tool. Once well-being needs are identified, the caseworker should give assistance to the family in meeting these needs by providing the information, services or referral to service providers to meet the needs. The actions taken by the caseworker to assist the family should also be noted in this section. Note the progress of the family and caseworker toward meeting the identified needs in the follow up/next steps section. Note: If a "well-being" issue deteriorates to the point that it meets the definition of abuse, neglect or dependency, then a new CPS report must be initiated. Remember that lack of adherence to the well-being issues is not a reason to initiate court proceedings against the parent if it is not seen as a risk/safety issue. Well-being issues alone are also not reasons to keep the case open when it would otherwise be closed for services. "If the child cannot be safely maintained in the home, what are the parents' preferences for placement?" must be answered for ALL children. If the child's parents have differing preferences, state each parent's preference. If out-of-home placement appears imminent, it becomes the caseworker's responsibility to assess any placement resource/safety resource to ensure that it is a safe and nurturing environment for the child using the Initial Provider Assessment. There is an asterisk that denotes the requirement to include the plan of removal if safety cannot be achieved. Parent or Caretaker Well-being Needs to be addressed with Optional Services

Only needs that are not significant enough to cause county child welfare involvement but if addressed could enhance the parent(s) ability to provide for his or her children should be listed in this section. An example may be a medical need that a parent has neglected but impacts the quality of daily living. The child welfare agency is required to identify such needs and offer to assist parents or caretakers in addressing them. Services listed are voluntary on the part of the family and offered at county option. Families have the right to refuse voluntary services for any reason. The agency cannot justify initiating involuntary services or court action based solely upon the client's refusal of voluntary/requested services. When applicable, complete the chart listing optional services, participants, and dates when developing the IHFCP. Provide updates as appropriate at each review.

Signatures The parent/caretakers participating in the development of the agreement and the child(ren) if cognitively and emotionally able to participate with the development of the agreement should be asked to sign the plan. By signing the agreement, the family, the caseworker, the child or children and any others who were involved with the development of the plan acknowledge their participation in the development and/or update of the case plan. The date of the signatures must be documented on the IHFCP, and a new signature page must be included for each formal update of the plan. A copy of the full case plan must be given to the parents/caretakers involved. The caseworker must document to whom copies of the plan were given. In domestic violence situations, the non-offending adult victim and perpetrator should sign separate case plans. The written plan with the adult victim should not be shared with the perpetrator. If a parent/caretaker declines to sign the IHFCP, the caseworker should try to address the caretaker's concerns and stress the need for working together to prevent the removal of the child(ren) from the home. The caseworker can highlight the statement above the signature lines that explains the signature indicates participation in the development or update of the IHFCP and agreement to be referred to services and participate in safety activities as listed. As such, the signature does not affirm full agreement with all statements in the plan. The caretaker may verbally agree to the case plan even if they decline to sign. The caseworker must check the box Parent declines to sign on the case plan and note whether each service and activity has been agreed to by the caretaker in their documentation if the caretaker declines to sign. If the caretaker declines to sign or verbally agree to the services in Section 2, the agency has the responsibility to ensure that the child is safe whether he is in his own home or in another type of placement. The child welfare agency can consider filing a petition for court intervention. Other service providers, community representatives, or family members and friends who are a part of the family's Safety Network may also sign. These signatures are optional and not required.
