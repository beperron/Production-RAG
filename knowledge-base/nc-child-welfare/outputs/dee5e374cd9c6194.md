TO: County Directors of Social Services

## SUBJECT: NEW LEGISLATION REGARDING ADOPTIVE

## FAMILIES AND CRIMINAL RECORDS CHECKS

Date: August 25, 2005

EFFECTIVE: IMMEDIATELY

ATTENTION: Children' s Services Program Managers

Children's Services Supervisors

I. GENERAL INFORMATION

The purpose of this letter is to inform you of legislative changes regarding fingerprint-based criminal records checks for adoptive families who are attempting to adopt a child who is in the custody or placement responsibility of a county Department of Social Services. Session Law 2005-114, House Bill 451 was passed by the 2005 General Assemble effective June 24, 2005. This is entitled: "An act to require criminal background checks for any individual eighteen years of age or older who resides in the household of a prospective adoptive parent, to expand the persons who are subject to criminal record checks by department of health and human services, and to make other conforming changes." This legislation amends GS 48-3-309 and GS 114-19.7.

This legislation brings North Carolina in compliance with the federal Child Abuse Prevention and Treatment Act (CAPTA) which now requires at all individuals living in the household of a prospective adoptive family who are eighteen years of age or older must have local, state and national criminal background checks. This means that before a child that is in the custody or placement responsibility of a county Department of Social Services can be place with a family for the purpose of adoption, there must be criminal background checks on each individual in the home that is eighteen years of age or older. Note that this legislation does not pertain to adoptions of children who are not in the custody or placement responsibility of a county Department of Social Services in North Carolina.

II. IMPLEMENTATION PROCEDURES

All agencies working with families who are applying to adopt a child who is in the custody of a county Department of Social Services must implement this law immediately. Effective immediately, you should submit the fingerprints and the required releases of information for all applicants for adoption and any individuals 18 years old or older who are to be living in the adoptive applicants' home to the DHHS Criminal Records Check Unit at the address below.
