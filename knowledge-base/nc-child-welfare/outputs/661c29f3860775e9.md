# PERMANENCY PLANNING FAMILY CASE PLAN (PPFCP) INSTRUCTIONS

# GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS

# Which Cases

The Permanency Planning Family Case Plan (PPFCP) must be completed for:

- All children/youth in the legal custody of a local child welfare agency (CWA).
- Children and youth for whom the local child welfare agency has placement responsibility through a Voluntary Placement Agreement (VPA) and are placed outside the home.
- Families with children/youth who are in a trial visit and the local CWA retains custody.

# Purpose

The purpose of the Permanency Planning Family Case Plan (PPFCP) is to:

- Develop a plan with the family that specifies expectations for behavioral change related to:
- danger indicators identified through Structured Decision Making (SDM) tools, and,
- needs identified in the Family Strengths and Needs Assessment (FSNA).
- Identify safety networks and build on the strengths identified in the FSNA to ensure the child(ren) has a safe, permanent home.
- Specify the agency actions that support progress toward safety, well being, and permanency, recognizing that the case plan reflects shared responsibility and must capture what the agency is doing—not only what the family is asked to do.

NOTE: One PPFCP is completed for the entire family except for cases:

- Involving domestic violence that require separate plans for the parents; or
- Where DSS has identified a safety issue that requires separate plans for different parties of the case; or
- Where family members request separate case plans (in PATH NC, this would require completion of 2 separate FSNA).

# Required Timeframes

The Permanency Planning Family Case Plan (PPFCP) must be:

- Completed within 30 days of removal of the child from the home.
- Reviewed (and updated, if needed) within 60 days of removal of the child from the home.
- Updated every 90 days thereafter (align with required Permanency Planning Reviews); and
- Updated within 30 days of the court's decision to change the child's permanent plan.

Case plans are intended to guide services and decision making so that each child can achieve permanency within a 12 month timeframe.

Case plans function as living documents because they are meant to evolve with the family’s progress, challenges, and changing circumstances. Caseworkers should be checking in with families regularly—not just at formal update points—to understand what is working, what is stalling, and what supports may need to shift. Ongoing conversations help ensure the case plan reflects the family’s real time experiences and keep everyone aligned on the actions that build safety and stability. This steady engagement also strengthens Child and Family Team meetings, allowing workers to come prepared with up to date information that guides meaningful discussion and clear next steps.

If the PPFCP is not completed within the required timeframes, caseworkers must document the rationale within the case narrative.

The PPFCP development and updates must be completed during a Child and Family Team (CFT) meeting, which includes the caseworker, the parents/caretakers, the child or youth as appropriate to age or developmental level, the child’s placement provider, and any other person(s) identified by the family. If the child or youth is a member or is eligible to be a member of a federally recognized Indian Tribe or is the biological child of a tribal member, a person appointed by and representing the tribe must be involved in the development of the case plan.

If the youth is 14 years of age or older, they must be consulted during the development of the case plan. The youth is allowed to appoint up to two members of the team who are not a resource parent or the youth's caseworker. If the agency rejects a youth’s selection, the rationale must be documented and free from bias.

# Non-Resident/Non-Offending Parents:

When either the primary or secondary plan is reunification, the activities to find, contact, and/or engage any nonparticipating parent must be identified on the PPFCP in the section “Objectives to Address Barriers to Identified Permanency Plans.” The agency must specify a barrier to reunification as “locate and engage the parent”. Activities entered to address that barrier should include, but are not limited to, specific actions the agency will take to:

- Locate the parent;
- Contact the parent;
- Assess parent's strengths, needs, and ability to provide for the child (FSNAs are not completed on non-maltreating parent/caretakers); and
- If appropriate or court-ordered, engage parent to develop a PPFCP.

Indicate how often the agency will make efforts to locate, assess, contact and engage the parent and who will be responsible for those activities.

# Preparing the Child, Family, and Placement Provider

The Permanency Planning Family Case Plan (PPFCP) should involve the child and family during planning, and recognizes each family has strengths and is unique. The worker should prepare the child, family, and placement provider about the case planning process ahead of the CFT meeting and discuss everyone’s role. Before the CFT meeting, the worker should discuss with the parent whether there are portions of the case plan—such as objectives related to substance use treatment—they prefer not to discuss in the presence of the placement provider due to confidentiality. Parents must be reminded that they have the right not to disclose substance use treatment information to others without their written consent, consistent with federal confidentiality protections under 45 C.F.R. Part 2.

When parents choose to share or restrict information, the worker documents the decision and ensures that a DSS 5297 Consent for Release of Information is completed and maintained in the case file.

# Development and Completion of Case Plan

One Permanency Planning Family Case Plan (PPFCP) is completed for the entire family. Exceptions outlined above in the “purpose” section.

The initial PPFCP must be developed during a Child and Family Team (CFT) meeting. Child and Family Teams (CFT) are family members and their community support that come together to create, implement, and update a plan for the child(ren), youth, and family. The purpose of a CFT meeting is to:

- Reach agreement on which identified child welfare issues will be addressed and how they will be addressed throughout the life of the case;
- Develop a case plan or safety plan that is created using the best ideas of the family, informal, and formal supports that the family believes in, the agency approves of, and lessens risk and heightens safety for the child/youth and family; and
- Plan for how all participants will take part in, support, and implement the case plan or safety plan developed by the team.

When parents are not present, the case plan must still be completed to meet federal case planning requirements under 42 U.S.C. § 675(1). In these circumstances, the meeting is not considered a CFT; instead, it is a case plan meeting. Conducting the meeting allows the agency to:

- Document diligent efforts to engage the parents in the planning process.
- Continue developing and updating the case plan to address the child’s safety, well being, and permanency needs.

# SECTION COMPLETION INSTRUCTIONS:

Select the review type:

- Initial Case Plan
- Case Plan Update
- Permanency Planning Review
- Case Plan Update and Permanency Planning Review

(Note: Both case plan update and permanency planning review types may be selected at the same time if objectives are being updated during the PPR. The PPR should NOT be completed at the same time as the initial case plan.)

# Family Information

To best utilize the meeting time, the family information section will be generated based on information already entered by the worker in PATH NC system prior to the meeting. The worker should review all fields to make sure they are current and update as needed. Add the plan participants.

# Family Strengths &#x26; Supports

Highlighting family strengths and supports is important when working with families to develop the PPFCP. By explicitly naming a family’s strengths and natural supports, caseworkers can empower families by grounding the case plan in the family’s own capacity to create safety.

In this section, workers will identify parent/caretaker strengths from the most recent FSNA, which can be auto-populated as long as the FSNA has been completed. Identification of natural supports and how these supports are involved with the family should also be captured along with any strengths not on the FSNA.

# Reasonable Efforts to Prevent Removal

Describe the services that were offered/provided to prevent removal. This includes any support the agency made available to the family, such as parenting classes, substance use treatment, mental health services, housing assistance, or in-home safety planning. When writing this narrative, specificity matters. Avoid vague statements like “services were offered.” Instead, name the service, the provider, and the outcome. It’s important to distinguish between what was offered and what was actually provided or accessed by the family. If services were declined or didn’t resolve the safety concerns, that should be documented as well.

# Objectives &#x26; Behavior Changes to Address Identified Needs and Barriers

The objectives/goals and activities must be developed in partnership with the family and written in the family’s terms. Objectives must be child-specific and identify the child(ren) that each objective applies to. One useful way to conceptualize the connection between case plans, objectives/goals, and the associated activities is to think of a map. Each goal or objective identified on the case plan represents a destination—that is, what must be true for the child to be safe. The activities aligned with each goal/objective serve as the directions that outline the steps required to reach that destination.

Each objective/goal on the Permanency Planning Family Case Plan (PPFCP) should be tied to a permanency plan identified by the court. If a permanency plan has not yet been established by the court, the objectives/goals should outline the agency identified permanency plan. Objectives/goals to support concurrent plans MUST be captured either in this section or “Objectives to Address Barriers to Identified Permanency Plans.”

The priority needs must be identified from the most recent FSNA. PATH NC is designed to populate 1 need per page on the case plan. Caseworkers should select the top 3 priority needs from the FSNA. If there are other needs that need to be addressed, they can be added manually after any of the top 3 priority needs are completed.

If there are court ordered services related to needs that are not identified as the 3 priority needs from the FSNA, these should be reflected in the section “Court Ordered Services and Recommendations for Parent/Child/Youth.”

# For Each Need: What Already Happened?

Document the harm statement here. Harm statements are clear and specific statements about the harm or maltreatment experienced by a child. Harm statements are composed of the following:

| What occurred                                                                                                           | What caretaker actions/inaction                     | Impact on the child |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Example: Sam's dad, Jerry, drank too many beers and got mad at his mom, Helen. Sam saw Jerry hit Helen across the face. | Sam felt really scared, cried, and hid in his room. |                     |

While it is never a guarantee, a clear understanding of the past (harm) is vital as our best guide to understanding what we should be worried about in the future.

# Describe Behaviors of Concern: What We Worry Will Happen Next?

Document the worry statement here. Worry statements answer two questions.

- What are we worried will happen to the children if nothing else changes?
- In what situations or context are we worried this could happen?

Sharing worry statements with the family, agency, and other professionals allows a sharper focus on key.

# Objective/Desired Outcome: How Will We Know If the Worry Is Resolved?

Document the goal statement here. Goal statements are short, simple, behavior-based statements used to help family members, departmental staff, and other professionals clearly understand what actions parents need to take to show that the child will be safe. Goal statements lay the groundwork for the family to successfully complete their case plan. They describe what the family can do to create safety for their child. As much as possible, try to use the family’s own language for these statements. Remember that the best use of these statements is to help ensure that all the key stakeholders—especially the family—are clear about where the family is headed with help from child welfare services. These statements should be written in honest, detailed, nonjudgmental “just-the-facts” language.

Goal statements should respond to the worry statements in about three or four sentences. Goal statements are composed of the following:

| Child        | What will be done differently                                                                                                                                        | To address the safety threat |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Example: Sam | will be cared for by adults who solve their disagreements and problems in loving and caring ways, treat each other respectfully, and ask for help when they need it. |                              |

# Activities Related to Identified Needs/Barriers

Activities should be behaviorally specific and clearly describe the observable actions. The activities should be written so that everyone involved in the case plan understands exactly what is expected. The activities section of the case plan should spell out who will do what, when, and how, so that each step clearly supports progress toward the goal/objective.

Agency activities should move past simple monitoring and referrals by actively partnering with the family—removing barriers, supporting the day to day work of change, and reinforcing the behaviors that build safety. This includes recognizing that services alone do not create safety; the agency’s role is to help.

# Objectives to Address Barriers to Identified Permanency Plans

Document any barriers preventing progress towards permanency and activities to address those barriers, such as obstacles not identified through the FSNA. Objectives/goals to support concurrent plans MUST be captured either in this section or “Objectives and Behavior Changes to Address Identified Needs.” Example: if a child’s permanency plan is adoption but no adoptive home has been identified, this section could outline efforts such as family finding activities or a referral for child-focused recruitment.

# Parent(s) Well-Being Needs/Additional Needs

The CFT should identify any additional needs of the parent(s) that are not identified in previous sections and describe how those needs will be addressed. Example: Transportation or employment - Although a lack of transportation or employment may not have been associated with the child(ren) coming into custody, either of these needs could significantly impact a parent's ability to accomplish activities identified in the PPFCP.

# Child/Youth Specific Plan, Well-Being Needs &#x26; Review

Many items in these sections will auto populate from other areas in PATH NC. Ensure the auto populated items are correct and update as needed.

# Court Ordered Services and Recommendations for Parent/Child/Youth

Ensure court ordered services and/or activities are incorporated into the PPFCP. If not, explain why. Provide the date of the next hearing and identify recommendations regarding the parent(s)/caretaker(s) services or barriers for the next court review.

# Placement Provider

Complete all questions related to the placement provider related to each child.

# Signatures

The signature page is to be signed by those who participated in the development of and any updates to the PPFCP. By signing, those involved with the development and updates of the case plan are acknowledging that they understand their role in the case plan and in meeting the identified needs. Document how the person participated (in person, virtual, or by phone), the date they participated, and if they received a copy. A copy of the PPFCP should be given to all parties involved in the development or updates of the PPFCP.

If a parent or caregiver refuses to sign the PPFCP, the worker should try to address the individual's concerns and stress the need for working together to reunify the child or youth with the family. The parent or caregiver may verbally agree to the case plan even if they refuse to sign the case plan. In this case, the social worker should select that the parent declined to sign and explain/document the extent of the parent’ caretaker’s participation.
