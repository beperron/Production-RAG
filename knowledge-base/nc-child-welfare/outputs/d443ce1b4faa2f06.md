|Is a Safety Network Member required when building a safety plan?|Selecting a danger indicator means that there is a safety issue identified. A<br>safety plan is what the family is going to do to mitigate the risk and allow<br>the children to remain in the home. The safety plan needs to include what is<br>going to happen and who is going to do it to ensure that children will be<br>safe in the home. The focus of the plan needs to be on what behavior will<br>be changed and who will make sure that change is going to happen OR<br>report it to CPS when it is not happening.<br>A Safety Network member is required anytime a danger indicator is<br>selected and a safety plan is being developed to allow the children to<br>remain in the home. The network member helps to build a safe plan where<br>caretakers can be held accountable to address immediate safety within<br>their own support network. This is designed to replace promissory notes<br>where caretakers have harmed their child, and it is assumed that they can<br>fix a behavior that they have not been given the tools or services to<br>change. The Network member does not have to live in the home but can if<br>they are not the offender and can maintain safety for the child. The Safety<br>Network member must be someone from the family's support circle<br>because CPS is not a long-term solution and this allows the family to<br>actively work towards their own safety without CPS.<br>If the family is struggling to identify a Safety Network member, use of the<br>Circle of Safety and Support resource can help workers support families in<br>identifying people who can be engaged and brought into the Network. This<br>may need to be a creative process in cases where the family has limited<br>supports but can be framed as a way to mitigate the need for further CPS<br>intervention.|
