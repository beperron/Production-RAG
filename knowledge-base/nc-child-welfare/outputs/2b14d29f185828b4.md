# NORTH CAROLINA SDM® TOOL DESK GUIDE

### PURPOSE

This document explains how North Carolina's Structured Decision Making® (SDM) assessments connect across

the life of a child welfare case and clarifies how SDM® terminology applies at different stages of work with

families. It is intended for staff training and supervision; shared understanding among staff, families and courts;

and consistent practice implementation.

### OVERVIEW: SDM SYSTEM ACROSS THE LIFE OF A CASE

The SDM system supports decision making from the time a report is received through permanency and case closure. Each assessment answers a specific question, and the language used should align with that purpose.

REPORT

Screening and Response Tool: Should this report be assessed (family or investigative)?

INVESTIGATIVE OR FAMILY ASSESSMENT

Safety Assessment: Can the child remain safely in the home?

Risk Assessment: Should a case continue with ongoing services?

Family Strengths and Needs Assessment: What goals should be addressed in the case plan?

ONGOING SERVICES

In-Home Cases

Family Strengths and Needs Assessment: What goals should be addressed in the case plan?

Progress to Case Closure Guide1: Should this case be closed?

Permanency Planning Cases

Family Strengths and Needs Assessment: What goals should be addressed in the case plan?

Reunification Assessment2: Can the child safely return home?

1 Assessment caseworkers do not complete case plans.

2 Assessment caseworkers do not complete case plans.

KEY PRACTICE PRINCIPLES • Safety decisions occur throughout the life of a case. • Behavior change matters more than participation or compliance. Service completion alone does not equal safety. • Case closure is a safety determination, not a checklist. Safety means BOTH the absence of danger indicators and the consistent, demonstrated protective actions of the caretaker(s) and their support network over time.

SAFETY-ORGANIZED PRACTICE Safety-Organized Practice (SOP) has three primary objectives. • Develop good working relationships • Use decision-support tools and critical thinking • Build collaborative plans that enhance daily child safety
